Question 1

Where is retail media heading?

Question contextOptional background for the speaker

This opening question sets the direction for the panel. Much of the audience works in consumer retail media and is likely to be interested in how the B2B perspective differs.

The audience will likely expect:

  • A clear view of where the market is moving
  • A concrete example that illustrates the B2B difference
  • Acknowledgement that B2B advertising is well established, while retail media for professionals is an emerging field
Short answer · about 45 seconds
Headline"Retail media follows the customer. And the customer that matters next is the professional."
Who buys"Our customers are HoReCa and traders. One hotel switching its breakfast coffee pours it for every guest, every morning."
How they buy"They don't only come to the store anymore. They order in the app, online and by delivery. One customer, many touchpoints."
What they want"Professionals don't want ads. They want ideas: what to put on the menu, what sells, what saves time."
Who decides"Soon the corner shop's weekly restock may be prepared by an agent. Agents read product data, price and availability, not banners."
METRO Wholesale Media"That's why we built METRO Wholesale Media: to follow the professional across every touchpoint, with messages that help their business."

Longer versionAbout 2 minutes
HeadlineRetail media always follows the customer. For years that meant the consumer. B2B has always advertised, through trade fairs, sales reps and trade promotions, but retail media for professionals, at the moment of purchase and based on real buying data, is still early. That's the next chapter.
Who buysAt METRO, our customers are restaurants, hotels, caterers and independent shops, and most of them are known, registered businesses. A hotel kitchen serves breakfast to hundreds of guests a day, and an independent grocer serves a whole neighbourhood. Reach one of our customers and you reach everyone they serve.
How they buyA hotel buyer still walks the aisles, but also orders in the app, online and through delivery. Across the industry, wholesalers are combining stores, digital and delivery. For a brand, that means one customer across many touchpoints, and media has to follow them.
What they wantProfessional buyers want help running their business: new dishes, what's selling locally, products that save staff hours. Brands that bring useful content will win, and because a business that switches can reorder for months, they'll judge media on customers won and kept.
Who decidesAI is starting to change who places the order. We believe routine reorders are among the first tasks people will hand to agents. So brands will need to reach two buyers: the professional, and the agent working for them.
METRO Wholesale MediaThat's the role of METRO Wholesale Media: following the professional customer across store, app, online and delivery, with messages relevant to their kind of business. Our ambition is to be the most trusted way to reach professionals at the moment they make a business decision.

Longer versionAbout 90 seconds
HeadlineWhen people talk about retail media opportunities, they usually mean more ad space. In wholesale, the bigger opportunity is growth: helping a business add a category, and helping a brand win that business.
MomentsProfessionals change what they buy at specific moments: a kiosk adds hot snacks, a hotel opens a rooftop bar, a bakery starts a lunch counter, a caterer prepares for wedding season. Those are the moments a brand can earn a place.
OccasionsA new cocktail menu needs spirits, syrups, glassware and ice, and METRO sells all of it under one roof. Brands that support the whole occasion together create more value for the customer and for each other.
ModelsThat opens different business models: paying for accounts won rather than impressions, funding benefits the customer values, and turning aggregated category trends into an insight product.
METRO Wholesale MediaAt METRO, Wholesale Media builds on relationships and mechanics that already exist, our supplier partnerships and promotion calendar, and connects them into something measurable. The real question isn't how much media we can sell. It's how much growth we can create.

Question contextOptional background for the speaker

As the closing question, this is an opportunity to leave the audience with clear and actionable priorities.

The audience will likely expect:

  • Two or three priorities the audience can take away
  • Recommendations that are constructive for the brands in the room

Short answer · about 45 seconds
Headline"Three priorities: one for brands, one for retailers, and one we can only do together."
1 · Brands"Treat B2B as its own channel. Ask for new business accounts, not just return on ad spend, and invest in product data, because in B2B the product page does the selling."
2 · Retailers"Earn trust before selling space. Professionals are at work, so every message has to help their business. Guardrails first, inventory second."
3 · Together"Fewer networks, shared standards. Brands don't want 40 logins and 40 ways to measure. They want one way to buy and results they trust."
METRO Wholesale Media"That's our 2027 commitment at METRO Wholesale Media: clear rules for our customers, simple booking for brands, and results they can believe. Because the winners won't have the most ad slots. They'll be the ones a chef trusts, and the chef's AI can read."
